  <section id="current-research" aria-labelledby="current-research-title">
    <h2 id="current-research-title">Current research</h2>
    <p>
      Two ongoing projects use sociocentric network data from the Korean Social Life, Health, and Aging Project (KSHAP), distinguishing the ties older
      adults report from the nominations they receive from others.
    </p>
    <article>
      <h3>Oral health, received ties, and cognitive aging</h3>
      <p class="research-status">KSHAP · In progress</p>
      <p>
        How is tooth loss related to being named as a discussion partner and to later cognitive function? Using two waves of KSHAP, this study examines
        received nominations (in-degree) as a potential pathway linking remaining teeth to later MMSE scores, alongside self-reported ties (out-degree).
      </p>
    </article>
    <article>
      <h3>Trajectories of social connectedness in later life</h3>
      <p class="research-status">KSHAP · In progress</p>
      <p>
        This project follows changes in outgoing ties (out-degree), ties outside the township, and nominations received from others (in-degree). It
        examines what these dimensions add to network trajectory patterns and how those patterns relate to later cognitive function.
      </p>
    </article>
  </section>

  <section id="under-review" aria-labelledby="under-review-title">
    <h2 id="under-review-title">Under review</h2>
    <article>
      <h3>Strong-Tie Social Capital and Life Satisfaction Across 30 Countries: The Moderating Roles of Individual Income, National Affluence, and Income Inequality</h3>
      <p class="research-status">Social Indicators Research · Under review</p>
      <p>
        Comparing social capital embedded in strong and weak ties across 30 countries, this study examines how its association with life satisfaction
        varies by individual income, national affluence, and income inequality.
      </p>
    </article>
    <article>
      <h3>Transitions in Social Network Types and Depressive Symptoms Among Older Korean Adults: A Longitudinal Study Before and During COVID-19</h3>
      <p class="research-status">Aging &amp; Mental Health · Under review</p>
      <p>
        Using three waves of the Korean Longitudinal Study of Ageing (KLoSA, 2018–2022), this study tracks changes in network types before and during the
        pandemic and examines their association with later depressive symptoms.
      </p>
    </article>
  </section>
